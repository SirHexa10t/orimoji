# Observations

What orimoji measured on real data. Why the numbers come out this way is in
[theory.md](theory.md); how they were taken is at the [end](#measured-how).

## The data

Two sibling projects gave orimoji its test data, each a kind of file it was made for:

- **catalogames' listing of Steam's catalogue**, `data/steam/steam-games.tsv` in the local copy of
  catalogames: 32,198,699 bytes, 188,759 lines, 20 tab-separated columns (app ids, review bands and
  counts, release dates, platform and language masks, tag lists, studios, names), with 58 comment
  lines at the top. A big, regular table, regenerated by a sweep now and then.
- **arterminal's three example drawings**, as arterminal saves them: a 13 KB ASCII-art poster
  ("long art"), a 1.1 KB braille drawing ("Kirino"), and a 6.4 KB drawing of coloured block
  elements ("Miku"), whose colours are terminal escapes. Small files, kept in a repository a line
  per row, which arterminal folds with its own *crumple*: canonical Huffman codes per row in
  base64, the approach orimoji's text and table squeezes grew from.

`examples/data` holds copies: the three drawings as arterminal ships them, and the listing's header
with its first 400 rows.

## catalogames' listing

| Squeeze, into base64url | Folded file | In git | A line per line | Fold / unfold |
|---|--:|--:|---|--:|
| `table --index` | 40% | 72% | yes | 1.7 s / 0.4 s |
| `table` | 76% | 117% | yes | |
| `text --index` | 69% | 107% | yes | 1.3 s / 0.4 s |
| `text` | 87% | 126% | yes | |
| `deflate`, leaning on the line above | 95% | 173% | yes | |
| `none` | 133% | 134% | yes | |
| `pack` | 205% | 385% | yes | |
| `deflate --whole` | 53% | 100% | no | |
| `zstd`, level 19 | 38% | 72% | no | 6.6 s / 0.1 s |

Sizes are against the file, an indexed fold's with its index, kept as text; *in git* is against what
git stores for the file itself (zlib, 12,896,515 bytes). Indexed, the table squeeze comes within two
points of zstd over the whole file, is about as small in git, and is still a line per row. Into
`two-byte` letters it is 48% of the file's bytes and 24% of its width. `pack` is the wrong tool
here: the listing uses hundreds of characters, so a place among them costs more than a byte.

Indexed, the table squeeze chose, column by column: distances for the sorted `app_id`; numbers for
`reviews`; item lists for `tagids` and `feats` (`,`), with lists of their common items; spelling for
`name`, whose repeats are too few to pay twice over; and for every other column, studios included, a
list of its common values with the rest spelled out. `orimoji fold --index` reports:

```text
orimoji: 32198699 bytes folded into 12491038 (39%), with table into base64url, a line for each line
orimoji: the index, 28365 values, is in listing.index
```

The index is 295 KB of text, kept beside the folded file.

On the 400-row excerpt in `examples/data` it chooses a little differently (`pubs` as `;` lists),
since what pays depends on how often values repeat; `cargo run --release --example steam_listing`
prints the choice for the excerpt, or for a listing given as its argument.

Refolding the listing with its own tables (`--like`) takes half a second and gives the same file,
byte for byte.

## arterminal's drawings

Into base64url:

| Squeeze | Long art, 13 KB | Kirino, 1.1 KB | Miku, 6.4 KB | A line per line |
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| arterminal's own crumple | 29% | 43% | 18% | yes |
| `text --index` | 31% | 63% | 22% | yes |
| `text` | 30% | 63% | 42% | yes |
| `table --index` | 36% | 68% | 31% | yes |
| `deflate`, leaning on the line above | 38% | 74% | 22% | yes |
| `pack` | 98% | 64% | 58% | yes |
| `deflate --whole` | 25% | 53% | 16% | no |
| `zstd` | 23% | 56% | 15% | no |

The indexed text squeeze comes within two to four points of crumple on the larger drawings, its
index included. On Miku the index is what does it: its colours are terminal escapes, and as single
symbols they halve the fold. On the long art it costs a little more than it saves (31% against
30%): the twice-over rule works on estimates, and a few words fell on the wrong side of it. On
Kirino nothing is worth indexing, and the code table outweighs the body itself, where crumple writes
its table in a few characters a symbol.

In git, none of these beats the drawing as it is: a small, repetitive drawing is what zlib
compresses best, and the indexed text squeeze's files take 121–144% of what git stores for the
drawings themselves (zstd's, 95–128%). Folding a drawing buys a smaller checkout and fewer
characters, not a smaller repository; the listing, big and regular, gets both.

## Lines leaning on lines

The drawings again, each line squeezed on its own or leaning on the lines above:

| Each line squeezed | Long art | Kirino | Miku | An edit changes |
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
| deflate, alone (`--lean 0`) | 48% | 87% | 46% | its line |
| deflate, leaning on the line above (`--lean 1`) | 38% | 69% | 21% | its line and the next |
| deflate, leaning on the 4 lines above | 36% | 63% | 20% | its line and the 4 after |
| deflate, leaning on the 16 lines above | 35% | 60% | 19% | its line and the 16 after |
| `text --index` | 31% | 63% | 22% | its line ¹ |
| arterminal's crumple | 29% | 43% | 18% | its line ¹ |
| *deflate over the whole file* | 24% | 47% | 15% | everything after it |

¹ And every line, if the edit rebuilds the shared code; not with `--like`.

The deflate rows count the body alone, without the folded file's header line. A code shared by the
whole file beats deflate leaning on its neighbours: deflate pays for its own framing on every line
and finds only what the lines above happen to repeat.

## What indexing buys

| | Without `--index` | With it |
|---|--:|--:|
| the listing, `table` | 76% | 40% |
| the listing, `text` | 87% | 69% |
| Miku, `text` | 42% | 22% |
| Miku, `table` | 41% | 31% |
| long art, `text` | 30% | 31% |
| Kirino, `text` | 63% | 63% |

Counted with its index, the index halves the fold where values or escapes repeat. Where nothing
repeats enough to pay twice over, as in the braille drawing, the fold says no index is needed and
is as it was; where little does, as in the ASCII art, it can cost a point.

## Measuring instead of estimating

Folded with `--measure-up-to`, the long art finds what the estimates missed: its index costs more
than it saves, so it is dropped, and the fold is back at 30%. On the listing, measuring confirms the
index: 12,786,159 bytes indexed, index included, against 24,487,121 without. Measuring the listing
takes 3.2 s against the plain fold's 1.6 s; given `--measure-up-to 100ms`, it gives up at its limit
and the fold takes no longer than without measuring.

## Memory

The listing (31 MiB) folded onto standard output, at the fold's peak:

| Fold of the listing | Time | Peak memory | With a buffer in tmpfs counted |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `none` | 0.1 s | 115 MiB | 119 MiB |
| `pack` | 1.1 s | 218 MiB | 222 MiB |
| `table --index` | 1.6 s | 177 MiB | 180 MiB |
| `table --index --measure-up-to 1h` | 3.2 s | 186 MiB | 202 MiB |
| the same, the indexed fold held in memory | 3.3 s | 205 MiB | 208 MiB |
| `auto --index` | 5.4 s | 270 MiB | 286 MiB |
| `auto --index --measure-up-to 1h` | 7.1 s | 270 MiB | 286 MiB |
| the same, the folds held in memory | 7.2 s | 331 MiB | 334 MiB |

At its peak a fold takes several times its input, most of it the squeeze's own work: the table
squeeze's model of every column, pack's numbers. `auto` peaks where `pack` does, the wrong tool for
the listing but still tried.

Measuring, the indexed fold (12.5 MB) waits in a temporary file while the fold without the index is
made, which keeps 19 MiB out of the fold's memory; through `auto`'s four folds and the measurement,
61 MiB. Here the file was in a tmpfs `/tmp`, so the bytes stayed in the machine's memory (the last
column): on a disk they leave it. The rows "held in memory" are the same build with nowhere to
write the file (a `TMPDIR` that doesn't exist), which it falls back from.

Two changes went with buffering. A fold used to hold its body twice at its end, as lines and as the
folded text: `pack` peaked at 380 MiB and `none` at 243 MiB. And `auto` used to fold the squeeze it
chose a second time: `auto --index` took 7.4 s and peaked at 413 MiB.

## One index for several files

The three drawings folded together share one index (`cargo run --example shared_index`): 6,211
bytes in all, against 6,235 with an index each. They have few values in common (the escapes are
the coloured drawing's alone), so sharing saves little here; it pays where files repeat each
other's values, as the sweeps of one listing do.

## Whole-file compressors

Before orimoji existed, a scratch benchmark compared compressors over the whole of each drawing,
base64 text of the result against the drawing, and their speed over 2 MB of Rust source:

| Compressor | Long art | Kirino | Miku | Compress, MiB/s | Decompress, MiB/s |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| deflate 6 | 24% | 47% | 15% | 107 | 1120 |
| deflate 9 | 24% | 47% | 15% | 56 | 1220 |
| zstd 3 | 27% | 53% | 19% | 360 | 1880 |
| zstd 19 | 22% | 50% | 14% | 7.3 | 1940 |
| brotli 11 | 22% | 44% | 14% | 1.3 | 800 |
| xz 9 | 23% | 52% | 16% | 5.6 | 230 |

orimoji has deflate and zstd: brotli and xz came within a few percent of zstd 19, far slower.

## Measured how

On one core of a Ryzen 9 9950X3D. The alphabets folded and unfolded 1 MiB of random bytes, best of
5. Peak memory is the fold's maximum resident set; with a buffer in tmpfs counted, the peak of its
memory cgroup, which charges tmpfs pages to the fold that wrote them. An indexed fold is measured with its index, kept as text. The listing is catalogames' local
`steam-games.tsv`, from its sweep of 2026-09-26. The drawings are arterminal's examples as
arterminal saves them. The size in git is zlib at level 6, git's default for packs.
`orimoji measure` made the result tables; the deflate rows under
[Lines leaning on lines](#lines-leaning-on-lines) and the whole-file compressors came from a
scratch benchmark of the same libraries, and crumple's sizes from arterminal itself.
